Planing Hydrodynamics

Savitsky Method for Planing Hulls

Empirical regression method — D. Savitsky (1964 & 1976)

The Savitsky method is the industry-standard empirical approach for predicting hydrodynamic performance of prismatic planing hulls at high speed. It establishes equilibrium between hydrodynamic lift, hydrostatic buoyancy, hull weight and thrust to solve running trim angle and wetted geometry.

Lift Equilibrium
$$W = N + B$$

N is hydrodynamic normal force and B is buoyant force. The method iterates trim angle $\tau$ until vertical and moment equilibrium are achieved.

Hydrodynamic Lift

The planing lift coefficient is a regression function of deadrise angle $\beta$, mean wetted length-to-beam ratio $\lambda$, and beam Froude number.

Planing Lift
$$C_L = C_{L0} - 0.0065\beta C_{L0}^{0.60}$$

Deadrise correction reduces lift for warped-section hulls with $\beta > 0^\circ$.

Skin Friction

Skin friction is based on the ITTC-1957 correlation line using mean wetted area. A roughness penalty is applied over smooth-plate friction to capture real-surface effects.

Validity Range

The method is valid for beam Froude numbers FnB ≥ 0.6, deadrise 0°–30°, and length-to-beam ratios 2–7. Below FnB = 0.6 the hull is outside the planing regime.
