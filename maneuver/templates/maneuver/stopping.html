Emergency Manoeuvring

Ship Stopping — Crash Astern

Two-phase propulsion reversal · IMO stopping criterion

The crash-stop manoeuvre is the standard emergency procedure for halting a ship in the shortest possible distance. The engine is rapidly reduced from full ahead to full astern, generating a counter-force that decelerates the vessel against its inertia and hydrodynamic resistance.

Thrust Schedule — Two Phases
$$ T_f(t) = \begin{cases} 1 - \dfrac{t}{t_{\text{red}}}, & t \leq t_{\text{red}} \\[6pt] T_{\text{rev}}, & t > t_{\text{red}} \end{cases} $$

tred — thrust reduction time. Trev — reverse thrust fraction.

Equations of Motion

The simulation integrates the full 3-DOF manoeuvring equations with a surge-direction propulsive term. Rudder angle is held at zero throughout, so lateral drift and heading change arise purely from hydrodynamic coupling as speed decays.

Key Stopping Metrics (IMO)
$$ S_{\text{stop}},\quad t_{\text{stop}},\quad \Delta y_{\text{max}},\quad U_{\text{final}} $$

Sstop — head-reach. tstop — time to stop. Δymax — maximum lateral deviation. Ufinal — residual speed at end of run.

IMO Stopping Standard

The IMO MSC Circular 1053 stopping criterion requires that a ship's stopping distance should not exceed 15 ship-lengths from full-ahead service speed.

How the Visualiser Works

Single ship · two-phase thrust · deceleration trajectory

Phase 1 Thrust Reduction

Thrust ramps linearly from full ahead to zero over the engine response time. The trajectory is drawn in amber.

Phase 2 Full Astern Thrust

The engine locks at the specified reverse fraction. The counter-thrust rapidly kills forward way. The trajectory is drawn in coral/red.

Drift Lateral Deviation

With zero rudder, residual sway and yaw produce lateral drift. The max deviation line is shown in teal.

IMO Check Stopping Distance

Head-reach is compared to the 15L IMO limit. A green / amber / red verdict is rendered in the telemetry panel.
